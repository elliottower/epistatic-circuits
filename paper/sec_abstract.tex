Circuit discovery methods in mechanistic interpretability identify
subgraphs of a neural network that explain a behavior, yet no existing
method provides the exact nonlinear interaction structure between
components. We show that the pairwise Walsh--Hadamard spectrum of a
transformer circuit --- the complete set of deviations from additivity
for every pair of attention heads --- can be recovered from as few as
$M \approx k$ random coalition evaluations via compressed sensing, where
$k$ is the number of coefficients and $2^n$ is the brute-force cost.
For a 20-head circuit in GPT-2 small, LASSO recovery reaches
correlation $r > 0.95$ at $M = 140$ ($7{,}490\times$ compression over
exact enumeration). Using this exact ground truth, we audit three
families of circuit discovery proxies. Weight geometry (composition
scores, subspace overlap) carries no cross-validated predictive power
for pairwise interaction under any ablation primitive. EAP edge
attribution agrees moderately with Walsh coefficients (Spearman
$\rho = 0.58$), capturing the linear component of interaction while
missing nonlinear routing effects. Path patching, which isolates direct
causal edges, is nearly orthogonal to Walsh interaction (signed Pearson
$r = 0.07$): the two methods measure categorically different quantities
--- functional coupling versus information flow. Finally, reading the
interaction spectrum against the documented IOI circuit taxonomy recovers
a sign-inversion dependency between S-inhibition and negative name-mover
heads that the original analysis recorded as unexplained. The pairwise
interaction structure is measurable, compressible, and carries mechanism
that additive circuit summaries discard.
