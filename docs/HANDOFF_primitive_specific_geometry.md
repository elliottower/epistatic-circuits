# Handoff — does weight geometry predict epistasis better under some ablation primitives?

For whoever picks this up. **Do not run the script as it stands.** Four fatal
objections are open and only two of twelve review findings have been addressed.
Everything needed to finish it is here, including the reasons not to.

---

## The question

The subspace analysis asked whether static weight geometry predicts head-pair
epistasis, and answered with one regression against epistasis measured under
**one** ablation primitive. Two facts already in hand say that treats three
quantities as one:

- Within a primitive, `W_ij` is measured precisely — prompt split-half on IOI c6
  gives Spearman–Brown **0.995** (`results/V13_RESULTS.md`).
- Across primitives it disagrees systematically — zero vs mean on the same
  circuit gives **r = 0.302**, and across 18 comparisons roughly **38% of pairs
  flip the sign** of their interaction
  (`results/LAB_NOTEBOOK_ablation_series.md`).

Precise measurement plus systematic disagreement means zero-, mean- and
resample-ablation epistasis are three distinct quantities. So "do the weights
predict epistasis?" has three answers and one was checked.

## Blockers, in the order they must be cleared

### 1. Provenance of the C6 selection — do this first, it is a grep

C6 head sets were chosen as **top 15 by order-2 Walsh energy** (`src/circuits.py`).
That is the quantity this regression predicts. Selection on signal-plus-noise
gives the *selecting* arm a winner's-curse component that no static-weight
predictor can capture, depressing its CV-R² by construction.

**If the LASSO-Walsh discovery ran under zero ablation, that alone produces P1's
predicted pattern with no LayerNorm story.** `src/circuits.py` does not record
the primitive and `results/RESULTS_SUMMARY.md` documents the criterion but not
the ablation. IOI c6 is the only clean 15-head circuit *and* the one most exposed
to this. Resolve before running.

### 2. Grouping key — **not fixed**

`scripts/modal_primitive_specific_geometry.py` derives the group from the
filename with no case-folding and no `_v5` stripping, so `ioi/c6` and
`ioi/C6_epistatic` — the same circuit — land in different groups, orphaning every
resample arm. There is also no `len(byabl) < 2` guard, so single-arm groups get
fitted and emitted, and the summary prints three medians over three different
circuit populations formatted exactly like a valid comparison.

Fix: key on `(task, frozenset(circuit_heads))`. **Task must be in the key** —
random circuits generated with the same seed have identical heads across tasks,
and keying on heads alone merges an IOI sweep with an RTI one. Add the two-arm
guard, and assert the contributing circuit sets are identical across arms before
writing any per-primitive aggregate.

### 3. P1's decision rule is not implemented — **not fixed**

The script emits per-primitive medians. P1 is a paired per-circuit rule. As it
stands the rule would be applied by hand after the rows are visible, over a
denominator the code determines post hoc, which is the freedom the freeze was
meant to close. Emit per-circuit arm differences, a fixed enumerated denominator,
and a boolean pass/fail.

Note `rng = np.random.default_rng(SEED)` sits inside the per-row loop, so all
arms of a circuit share head draws — common random numbers, ideal for a paired
difference, currently unused.

### 4. The bootstrap is not a bootstrap — **not fixed**

`keep = set(rng.choice(uh, len(uh), replace=True).tolist())` discards
multiplicity. Fifteen heads drawn with replacement give ~9.5 distinct, so every
replicate evaluates a ~9-head circuit rather than a 15-head one. CV-R² is
strongly size-dependent in that range, so the intervals will sit below the point
estimate they are attached to. Replicates failing the guards are also dropped
non-randomly.

Use a delete-one-head jackknife, or m-out-of-n subsampling with fixed m and the
√(m/n) correction, and log attempted vs dropped.

### 5. Fixed already

- **A1** cross-validation is now leave-both-heads-out: for pair (i,j), train only
  on pairs (a,b) with a,b ∉ {i,j}. No head shared between train and test.
- **A3** every row records `value_key` and `n_prompts`.

## Other review findings worth carrying

- **Arms differ in reliability.** Resample carries a sampling-noise term zero and
  mean lack. R² is bounded by target reliability, so a between-arm R² difference
  may just be a reliability difference. The script destroys the data needed to
  check — `ld.mean(1)` collapses per-prompt values on load. Add per-arm prompt
  split-half reliability and report R²/reliability alongside raw R².
- **No layer-distance-only baseline.** `|Δl|` is position, not geometry, and
  `q`/`k` are structurally zero for same-layer pairs, so they partly re-encode
  it. Report CV-R² for `[|Δl|]` alone and the increment from adding `[ov,q,k]`.
  This decides whether the paper's noun is "geometry" or "depth."
- **Features are not "unchanged"** as the prereg claims. The original collapsed
  to `[ov, max(q,k both orders), Δl]` = 3 predictors; this uses `[ov, q, k, Δl]`
  = 4. The prereg's null-model figure of 0.25 assumes 5 predictors; for 4 at 21
  pairs it is 0.20.
- **P4 compares incomparable numbers.** The published 0.068 was pooled over 14
  resample circuits against |W_ij| with 3 predictors. Score P4 against the
  `insample_r2` column this script produces instead.
- **P3 is not implemented** and cannot be scored from the current output.
  Persisting per-pair records (`circuit, primitive, i, j, ov, q, k, Δl, w`) makes
  P3, the layer baseline and any robustness request computable without re-running.
- **Abort condition 1 compares ordered lists**, not sets, so identical head sets
  in a different order are dropped as a mismatch. Abort condition 3 is not
  implemented at all.
- **The mechanism story needs weakening.** "Mean ablation preserves the norm" is
  not right — by Jensen the mean head output is systematically shorter than a
  typical one, and for a near-zero-mean head, mean ablation approaches zero
  ablation. The effect is real at the large-coalition end; say where it lives
  rather than asserting it globally.

---

## Recommendation

Run on the six clean circuits first — it is minutes of compute over sweeps that
already exist, and it says whether anything here is worth confirming. Only if
mean/resample come out clearly better predicted than zero is it worth forty
GPU-hours re-running RTI at matched prompts to make it a registered result.

Cheap look, then expensive confirmation. And clear blocker 1 before either,
because if C6 was selected under zero ablation then the one clean 15-head circuit
cannot carry the claim regardless of how well the code is fixed.
